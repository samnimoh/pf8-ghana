Abstract
Objective. To quantify spatial population structure, gene flow and candidate signatures of local selection in
Plasmodium falciparum across Ghana's agro-ecological zones, using the MalariaGEN Pf8 release, and to
establish whether zone-level differentiation is detectable at all before interpreting any locus-specific signal.
Data. Pf8 contains 33,325 samples and 12,493,205 variant sites. Ghana contributes 6,653 samples, of which
4,905 pass the release's own quality control. Isolates were collected between 2009 and 2021 and are
geolocated only to administrative level 1, giving seven distinct coordinates across seven regions. Spatial
joining to a Ghana agro-ecological zone layer placed these in four zones: Guinea savanna (n = 3,605),
deciduous forest (n = 815), coastal savanna (n = 435) and transitional zone (n = 50). The Sudan savanna and
the wet-evergreen and moist-evergreen forest zones are unrepresented.
Methods. Genotypes were obtained from the Pf8 Zarr store over HTTPS, restricted to Ghanaian samples,
sites passing the release filter, biallelic single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) and the GT, AD, DP and GQ
fields. Sample quality control applied a within-host fixation index (F ) threshold of 0.95, a call-rate floor of
ws
0.80, a heterozygosity outlier rule and clonal pruning. Mixed calls were resolved to the majority allele by read
depth, and allele counts were taken as one allele per isolate. Analyses comprised three ordinations of the Pf8
genotype-distance matrix (UMAP, t-SNE, PCoA), pairwise Hudson F with a block jackknife, ADMIXTURE at K =
ST
1–8 with cross-validation, Mantel tests of isolation by distance at zone and region level, TreeMix with 0–5
migration edges, and a BayPass XtX genome scan calibrated against 100,000 pseudo-observed neutral SNPs.
Principal findings. After all filters, 3,359 SNPs across 1,900 isolates entered the population analyses. Zone
differentiation is very low: the largest pairwise F is 0.0024 (95% CI 0.0014–0.0034) between coastal and
ST
Guinea savanna, and two of six pairs have confidence intervals spanning zero. Isolation by distance is
supported at region level (Mantel r = 0.400, p = 0.029, 7 populations, 21 pairs) but not at zone level (r =
0.578, p = 0.090, 4 populations, 6 pairs), where only 24 distinct permutations exist. ADMIXTURE cross-
validation error is minimised at K = 2 (0.83736) but the margin over K = 1 (0.84199) is small and ancestry
proportions are nearly uniform across zones. TreeMix fit improves monotonically to a plateau at three
migration edges, beyond the two that four populations can identify. The BayPass scan returns 15 outlier SNPs
above a calibrated XtX threshold of 11.23, intersecting 11 genes including the amino-acid transporter AAT1
(PF3D7_0629500, two outlier SNPs, maximum XtX 24.44). No validated antimalarial resistance locus appears
among the outliers.
Current conclusion. The Ghanaian parasite population behaves as a largely panmictic unit at the resolution
these data permit, with a weak distance-related signal that is detectable at region level. The analysis cannot
currently separate ecological-zone effects from administrative-region effects, because three of the four zones
are each filled by a single region; nor can it separate spatial from temporal effects, because sampling year is
strongly confounded with zone. The outlier set is a provisional candidate list, not a demonstration of local
adaptation. These results should be treated as the baseline characterisation that the marker-level thesis
questions depend on, not as answers to them.

1. Introduction and background
1.1 The problem
Malaria transmission in Ghana is spatially heterogeneous, and the heterogeneity tracks ecology. The country
spans a south–north gradient of agro-ecological zones that differ in rainfall, vector species composition,
seasonality and consequently in entomological inoculation rate. If parasite populations in these zones are
demographically separate to any meaningful degree, then drug-resistance alleles, antigenic variants and
locally adaptive alleles can differ in frequency between them, and a national surveillance programme built on
a handful of sentinel sites will mis-estimate the national picture. If instead the zones exchange parasites
freely, a resistance allele arising anywhere will spread everywhere, and the surveillance question becomes
one of timing rather than geography.
The two scenarios demand different control responses, and they are distinguishable from genomic data. The
present analysis addresses the prior question that both depend on: how much spatial structure is there in
Ghanaian P. falciparum, and is it enough to support zone-level inference at all?
Scope. The thesis proposal and literature review (supplied separately as "genomic analysis of Consistent signatures
of selection.docx", 145 paragraphs, approximately 11,400 words) set out the biological rationale, the resistance-
marker panel and the published context. This report does not restate that material. It documents what was
computed, on what data, with which decisions, and what the output actually shows.

1.3 Research questions as operationalised
The thesis questions were translated into the following testable forms. The translation is itself an analytical
decision and is revisited in Section 5.
1. Is between-zone differentiation distinguishable from zero? Operationalised as pairwise Hudson FST with
block-jackknife confidence intervals.

2. Does genetic distance increase with geographic distance? Operationalised as a Mantel test of FST/(1 −
FST) against log geographic distance, run at both zone and region level.
3. Do individual isolates assign to zone-associated ancestry components? Operationalised as ADMIXTURE
at K = 1–8 with 10-fold cross-validation, and as visual inspection of three ordinations.
4. Is there evidence of gene flow beyond a simple bifurcating population tree? Operationalised as TreeMix
with 0–5 migration edges.
5. Which loci are more differentiated than neutral demography predicts? Operationalised as a BayPass XtX
scan with a threshold calibrated on pseudo-observed data simulated under the estimated population
covariance matrix.
[Interpreted] Questions 1–4 are answerable at the resolution these data permit and are answered,

list; with four populations and 3,359 SNPs the scan has limited power, and a negative result for any particular
locus carries little information.

2. Data and materials
2.1 Source and provenance
[Observed] All genotype and metadata inputs derive from the MalariaGEN Plasmodium falciparum Community
Project release 8 (Pf8), served publicly over HTTPS from https://pf8-release.cog.sanger.ac.uk. The release
comprises 33,325 samples and 12,493,205 variant sites called against the 3D7 reference assembly (contigs
Pf3D7_01_v3 … Pf3D7_14_v3, plus apicoplast and mitochondrial contigs, which this analysis excludes).
Four Pf8 artefacts were used:
• Sample manifest — data/Pf8_samples.txt, 33,325 rows and 17 columns, giving country, administrative
level 1, latitude and longitude at that level, collection year and the release's own QC-pass flag.
• Variant call data — the Pf8 Zarr store at /zarr, holding GT, AD, DP, GQ and per-site annotations including
FILTER_PASS and RegionType.
• Mean genotype distance matrix — data/Pf8_mean_genotype_distance.npy, a 33,325 × 33,325 single-
precision matrix published with the release (4.44 GB).
• Reference annotation — data/PlasmoDB-71_Pfalciparum3D7.gff, used for Step 9. Release 68 is also
present in data/ but config.py points at release 71.
A Ghana agro-ecological zone polygon layer (data/ghanaAEZ.geojson) was supplied with the project. Its
provenance was not recorded in this session.
Provenance gap. [Unknown] The origin, vintage and coordinate reference system documentation of
ghanaAEZ.geojson could not be established from material available here. The file carries eight zone polygons
(coastal savanna, deciduous forest, Guinea savanna, moist evergreen, Sudan savanna, transitional zone, wet
evergreen, and a Volta Lake water body). The spelling "Decidous forest" in the layer is a misspelling that propagates
through every downstream output and zone label; it was deliberately left uncorrected so that zone keys remain

[Observed] Pf8 geolocates isolates to administrative level 1. Every isolate from a given region therefore carries
the identical coordinate pair, and the 4,905 QC-passing Ghanaian isolates occupy exactly seven distinct
points. Step 2 prints this explicitly: "4,905 samples occupy 7 distinct coordinate pairs."

3.2 Stage 1 — manifest filtering
What and why. The manifest is restricted to Ghana and then to isolates flagged QC-pass by the release.
Relying on the release's flag rather than re-deriving sample quality from raw data is a deliberate deference to
MalariaGEN's own curation, which has access to sequencing-run level information this analysis does not.
Implementation note. The script captures each retained isolate's row index in the full 33,325-sample
distance matrix as the column pf8_row. This is the only safe way to slice the Ghanaian block later: the pandas
index would shift under any subsequent filter, whereas pf8_row is fixed at the moment of extraction. Step 3

asserts that the maximum pf8_row does not exceed the matrix dimension, which would catch a Pf7/Pf8

[Observed] 6,653 Ghanaian rows → 4,905 after the QC filter (1,748 dropped). pf8_row range 2,301–29,498.
3.3 Stage 2 — agro-ecological zone assignment
What and why. Isolate coordinates are joined to the zone polygon layer with GeoPandas, giving each isolate
a zone label. Because the coordinates are region centroids, the join is effectively a lookup table from seven
points to four zones.
Assumption. That a region's centroid is a reasonable proxy for where its isolates were collected. For compact
regions this is tolerable; for regions that straddle a zone boundary it is not. The script prints a region × zone
cross-tabulation specifically so that boundary-straddling regions can be checked by eye, and flags Brong
Ahafo (forest/transition) and Volta (coastal/Guinea savanna) as the two warranting manual review.
[Observed] All 4,905 isolates matched exactly one polygon; zero unmatched, zero duplicate matches. Resulting
counts: Guinea savanna 3,605, deciduous forest 815, coastal savanna 435, transitional zone 50.
[Proposed] The Brong Ahafo and Volta assignments have not been manually reviewed against the map. Volta
in particular contributes 21 isolates to the deciduous forest zone, and its centroid sits near a boundary in the
layer.
3.4 Stage 3 — three ordinations of the genotype distance matrix
What and why. Pf8 publishes a precomputed mean genotype distance matrix over all 33,325 samples. The
Ghanaian block (4,905 × 4,905) is sliced out by memory-mapping the 4.44 GB file and taking the submatrix,
which keeps peak memory in the low hundreds of megabytes. Three dimensionality-reduction methods are
then applied to the same matrix so that they can be compared on equal terms.
• UMAP (n_neighbors = 30, min_dist = 0.1, metric = precomputed) — preserves local neighbourhood
structure with partial global layout. These are the parameter values specified in the thesis workflow.
• t-SNE (perplexity = 30, metric = precomputed, init = random) — preserves local neighbourhoods only.
Initialisation must be random rather than PCA-based because the input is a distance matrix rather than
feature vectors.
• PCoA (classical metric multidimensional scaling) — Gower double-centring followed by an
eigendecomposition. Unlike the other two it is linear, so axis scaling is interpretable and each axis carries
a proportion of variation explained.
Comparison metrics. Because the three methods optimise different objectives, a visual comparison alone
would be uninformative. Three quantitative measures are computed on the same embeddings:
trustworthiness (do points that are close in two dimensions correspond to genuine near neighbours in the full
matrix?), the Spearman correlation between pairwise distances in the embedding and in the original matrix
on a 2,000-sample subset (does the embedding preserve global structure?), and the silhouette coefficient by
zone (do the zone labels separate?). The silhouette is also computed on the original distance matrix, which
gives the ceiling that any embedding could achieve.

3.5 Stage 3a — genotype retrieval
What and why. This stage did not exist in the supplied pipeline and was written during this session to replace
a failed VCF download (Section 5.1). It fetches genotypes directly from the Pf8 Zarr store over HTTPS, chunk
by chunk, retaining only what the downstream steps read.
The Zarr store is chunked 131,072 variants × 100 samples per field. Because the 6,653 Ghanaian samples fall
in 77 of the 334 sample-chunk columns, and the sites of interest in 95 of the 96 variant-chunk rows, only a
subset of the grid must be transferred. Each chunk is downloaded, decompressed in memory, sliced to the
Ghanaian columns and the retained rows, then written to a local store and discarded, so peak memory stays
at a few gigabytes and the ~300 GB transferred is never written to disk.
Sites are retained where FILTER_PASS is true, is_snp is true and numalt equals 1. Fields retained are GT, AD
(reference and first alternate only), DP and GQ.
[Observed] 2,190,026 of 12,493,205 Pf8 sites are PASS biallelic SNPs. Every one of them carries RegionType =
"Core", confirmed by a direct tabulation. The transfer moved 299.8 GB in 3.20 hours at approximately 26
MB/s with zero failed requests, producing a 20.57 GB local store.
Consequence for the core-genome filter. The supplied pipeline expects an optional BED file of core-genome
intervals (data/pf_core_regions.bed), absent here, and warns that without it subtelomeric var/rifin regions
will contaminate the outlier scan. The tabulation above resolves that concern empirically: because all PASS

3.7 Stage 4a — building the genotype matrix
This stage converts the Zarr store into the single filtered genotype matrix that every subsequent stage reads.
The order of operations matters and is deliberate:
6. Site-level filters on the small one-dimensional arrays (FILTER_PASS, is_snp, numalt = 1), screening the
whole genome before any genotype is fetched.
7. Genotype-level masking: calls below a read-depth floor are set to missing. A low-confidence call thus
becomes missing data rather than a confident wrong answer, which matters because the missingness
filter at step 5 then sees it for what it is.
8. Sample alignment: genotype columns are reordered to match the metadata table, so that sample order
is fixed once and never re-derived.
9. Mixed-call resolution: heterozygous calls are replaced by the allele with more reads; ties become
missing (Section 5.3).
10. Variant missingness filter (≤ 10 per cent), then sample missingness filter (≤ 20 per cent), then minor
allele frequency filter (≥ 5 per cent). Missingness is applied before MAF so that a SNP is not retained on
the strength of a handful of confident calls.
Thresholds. MAF > 5 per cent and missingness < 10 per cent are specified in the thesis workflow document
for Step 4 and were not altered. The depth floor and the sample-missingness bound are pipeline defaults; the
depth and quality settings were revised during this session and that revision is documented in Section 5.5.
3.8 Stage 4b — pairwise differentiation
Estimator. Hudson's F is computed per locus and summed as a ratio of averages — numerator and
ST
denominator accumulated across loci before dividing — rather than as an average of per-locus ratios, which
is upwardly biased. Hudson's estimator is preferred over Weir and Cockerham's here because it is insensitive
to unequal sample sizes between populations, which is pertinent when one zone has 1,334 isolates and
another has 25.
Uncertainty. Standard errors come from a block jackknife over contiguous blocks of SNPs, which accounts for
linkage between nearby sites. Blocks are 2,000 SNPs by default, reduced automatically to at most one
twentieth of the available SNPs.

PAGE 18
Ploidy handling. Allele counts are taken as one allele per isolate (Section 5.3). This matters: counting both
copies of a diploid-coded haploid genotype would double every sample size entering the F correction, the
ST
TreeMix drift standard errors and the BayPass XtX statistic, making all three anticonservative.
3.9 Stages 5 to 9
ADMIXTURE (Stage 5). Genotypes are exported to PLINK transposed text format, converted to binary, LD-
pruned (50-SNP window, 10-SNP step, r² threshold 0.2) and run through ADMIXTURE for K = 2 to 8 with 10-
fold cross-validation. Pruning is not optional: linked SNPs carry redundant information and inflate apparent
structure. K = 1 was run separately during this session to provide the no-structure reference point that the
supplied sweep omits.
Mantel test (Stage 6). Isolation by distance is tested by regressing linearised differentiation, F / (1 − F ), on
ST ST
the logarithm of great-circle distance computed on the WGS84 ellipsoid. Significance comes from a
permutation test on the distance matrices (999 permutations, one-sided, since isolation by distance predicts
a positive association). The test is run at two levels: four zones, and seven administrative regions.
TreeMix (Stage 7). A maximum-likelihood population graph is fitted with 0 to 5 migration edges, rooted on
Guinea savanna, using a jackknife block size of 50 SNPs. Model fit is summarised as the proportion of variance
in between-population covariance explained, computed over off-diagonal entries only, matching TreeMix's
own definition.
BayPass (Stage 8). The core model estimates the population covariance matrix Ω from genome-wide allele
counts, then reports XtX per SNP — a differentiation statistic standardised by that covariance structure. A
SNP is called an outlier when its XtX exceeds a threshold taken from pseudo-observed data simulated under
the estimated Ω. The logic is that a threshold drawn from the empirical XtX distribution assumes a fixed
proportion of the genome is under selection, whereas a threshold from simulated neutral data assumes only
that the demographic model is adequate.
Annotation (Stage 9). Outlier positions are intersected with protein-coding gene features from PlasmoDB-71
using bedtools, and cross-referenced against a panel of eight validated antimalarial resistance loci and
against antigenic and invasion gene families.

outliers in a dispersed distribution; applied to a tight one it becomes an arbitrary strict threshold.

Three biases are introduced or amplified by the filtering, in decreasing order of concern.
11. Transmission-intensity bias (from the Fws filter). The retained sample under-represents high-

zone comparisons.
12. Regional bias (from the Pf8 QC flag and from the sampling frame itself). Upper East dominates every
stage. No filter corrects this and none was intended to.

13. Allele-frequency bias (from the MAF filter). The analysis sees only the common end of the frequency

common variants, Upper East–heavy — and conclusions should be stated as applying to that view.

5.1 Replacing the VCF route with direct Zarr retrieval

Problem and decision. The thesis workflow specifies UMAP. The user asked for alternative ordinations so

of the UMAP plot, and would not have been available from the specified workflow alone.
5.3 Ploidy: how to count alleles in a haploid parasite coded as diploid
Problem. Blood-stage P. falciparum is haploid, but Pf8 records genotypes in diploid form: 0/0 or 1/1 for a
clonal call, 0/1 where reads support both alleles (a mixed infection). The supplied step04b_fst.py called scikit-
allel's count_alleles on the diploid array, which counts two gene copies per isolate. The same cached counts
feed TreeMix and BayPass.
Why this is a defect, not a convention. Hudson's F carries a sample-size correction; TreeMix derives drift
ST
standard errors from counts; BayPass's XtX is standardised by them. Doubling every sample size makes all
three anticonservative — narrower intervals, more apparently significant edges, more apparent outliers —
without changing the point estimates much.
Options put to the user.
• Haploid, mixed calls to missing — use the pipeline's existing --het-to-missing switch, then count one
allele per isolate. Simplest; discards the mixed calls entirely.
• Haploid, mixed calls to the majority allele (chosen) — assign the allele with more reads, ties to missing,
then count one allele per isolate. Retains the information in the read depths.
• Leave as written — point estimates barely move, but uncertainty stays understated throughout.
Implementation and verification. A function resolve_mixed_calls was added to step04a_prepare_callset.py,
reading AD a block at a time only for SNPs where some retained isolate carries a mixed call. A companion
isolate_allele_counts was added to callset_io.py; it takes the first gene copy once mixed calls are resolved,
and falls back to diploid counting with an explicit warning if any mixed call remains. Both were verified
against a hand-computed synthetic case (3 SNPs × 4 isolates, including a tie and a missing genotype) before
being applied to real data.
[Observed] On the real dataset, 194,167 mixed calls were resolved: 129,665 to reference, 55,582 to alternate,
and 8,920 tied and set to missing. The residual heterozygous call rate printed by Step 4a is 0.0000 to four
decimal places, consistent with the F filter having already removed most mixed infections.
ws
[Interpreted] The asymmetry — 2.3 reference calls for every alternate call — is expected, since at a site where
most isolates carry the reference allele, a mixed infection is more likely to be reference-dominant. It is not
evidence of a reference bias in the resolution rule itself, though that possibility has not been formally
excluded.

5.5 TreeMix jackknife block size
Problem. The supplied configuration used 500 SNPs per jackknife block. With 3,359 SNPs that yields 6 or 7
blocks, far too few for stable standard errors, which in turn makes the migration-edge tests unreliable.
Decision. Reduced to 50 SNPs, giving approximately 67 blocks. With retained SNPs averaging roughly 7 kb
apart, a 50-SNP block spans about 350 kb, which comfortably exceeds the scale over which linkage
disequilibrium persists in P. falciparum. The block therefore remains large enough to absorb linkage while
being small enough to give a usable number of replicates. A 100-SNP alternative (about 34 blocks) was
offered and declined.
5.6 BayPass calibration
Problems found. Three, in the supplied step08b_baypass.sh.
14. The pseudo-observed data simulation passed the .geno matrix directly as sample.size. That file
interleaves reference and alternate counts, giving 2 × npop columns, whereas simulate.baypass requires
npop columns of total haploid sample size per SNP. The call would have failed, and the script would have
fallen through to an empirical quantile.
15. Only 1,000 pseudo-observed SNPs were simulated. The threshold is the 0.999 quantile of their XtX,
so it would have rested on a single simulated value.
16. The simulation applied no minor-allele-frequency filter, while the observed SNPs had been filtered at
MAF ≥ 5 per cent. The resulting neutral distribution was not comparable to the observed one: 17.0 per
cent of simulated sites had overall allele frequency below 0.05, against 0.5 per cent of observed sites.
Corrections. Reference and alternate columns are now summed to total counts; the simulation uses 100,000
SNPs; and pi.maf is set to 0.05 to match the observed filter. A stale G.pod file is removed before each run,
and an R failure now falls through to the documented empirical fallback instead of aborting the stage.
[Observed] The correction moved the threshold materially: from 8.375 with 1,000 unfiltered simulated SNPs
(40 outliers, 1.19 per cent of the scan) to 11.232 with 100,000 MAF-matched ones (15 outliers, 0.45 per cent).
The reported results use the corrected calibration.
[Interpreted] The direction is as expected: an unfiltered simulation contains rare sites whose XtX is low, which
depresses the upper quantile and admits too many outliers. The 25 SNPs that fall between the two
thresholds should be regarded as having failed calibration, not as marginal.

Why the smaller correlation is the stronger result. The zone-level test has the larger correlation but the
larger p-value, which is a power effect, not a contradiction. With four populations only 4! = 24 distinct
orderings exist, so the finest attainable p-value is 0.042 and significance requires essentially the single best
permutation. With seven regions there are 5,040 orderings and the test can resolve p down to 0.001. The

[Interpreted] Taken with Section 6.4, the picture is of a continuously distributed population in which
geographic distance has a weak but detectable effect on differentiation — the classic isolation-by-distance
signature — rather than of discrete populations separated by barriers. Note that this says nothing about
ecological zones as such: distance alone accounts for the pattern, and nothing in these data requires an
ecological explanation.
Caveat on the region-level test. [Interpreted] The region-level FST values are computed from the same 3,359 SNPs
but with populations defined by administrative region. Volta contributes 21 isolates and Brong Ahafo 25, so several
of the 21 pairs rest on small samples; five pairs in Figure 6.4 sit at or very near zero differentiation. The scatter is

[Interpreted] This is not a surprising result, and that is a point in its favour. Ghana is approximately 670 km

obligate sexual recombination in the mosquito, and transmission across most of the country is high. All four
favour genetic homogenisation. The finding is consistent with the continental picture, in which West African
parasite populations form a single major subpopulation (Amambua-Ngwa et al., 2019), and with the
observation that differentiation within West African countries is weak relative to the differentiation between
endemicity strata that Mobegi et al. (2014) reported between The Gambia and Guinea.
7.2 What the analysis cannot establish, and why
Zone effects cannot be separated from region effects. Three of four zones are each filled by exactly one
administrative region (Table 2.2). For those three, "zone" and "region" are the same variable. Any difference
attributed to ecology could equally be attributed to the region — its health system, its drug supply, its
sampling protocol, its collection year. No statistical adjustment can separate variables that are collinear by
construction.
Spatial effects cannot be separated from temporal ones. Median collection year differs by six years across
zones (Table 2.3), and for the transitional zone and coastal savanna the year ranges barely overlap. Over 2009
–2021 Ghana changed first-line therapy and scaled up chemoprevention, so drug-related allele frequencies
would be expected to shift with time regardless of geography.
Barrier hypotheses cannot be tested as such. Testing whether a specific feature (the forest–savanna
transition, the Volta Lake system) impedes parasite dispersal requires differentiation to be measurable across
the feature and compared against differentiation at the same distance elsewhere. With four populations, six
pairwise comparisons and F values under 0.0024, there is no resolution to work with.

7.3 Unexpected observations
The MAF ceiling. That the whole Ghanaian genome yields only about 5,500 common SNPs was not
anticipated and materially constrains every downstream analysis. It is a property of P. falciparum population
genomics within a single country — an abundance of rare variation, comparatively little common variation —
rather than a defect. But it means that a study of this design has considerably less power than the sample size
of 1,900 isolates would suggest.
Polyclonality as the clearest ecological signal. The one variable that orders cleanly along the north–south
gradient is the fraction of mixed infections (0.597 in Guinea savanna to 0.444 in coastal savanna). This is a
transmission-intensity signal, and it is arguably more informative about ecology than anything in the
population-genetic results.
[Hypothesized] If polyclonality tracks transmission intensity and transmission intensity tracks ecological zone,
then F is itself a candidate zone-level phenotype worth analysing in its own right rather than used only as a
ws
QC filter. That analysis has not been performed.
Likelihood monotonicity in TreeMix. The log-likelihood rises with every added migration edge, which is
expected but means likelihood cannot select a model. The supplied script's selection rule would not have
caught this; the defect is documented in Table 5.2.
7.4 Practical significance
[Interpreted] For malaria control the operational implication of near-panmixia is clear and, in its way, useful. If
Ghanaian parasites form one population, then a resistance allele established anywhere will spread nationally,
and sentinel surveillance at a small number of sites is an adequate design for detecting emergence — the
samples do not need to be spatially comprehensive to be representative. The corollary is less comfortable:
containment of an emerging resistance focus by geographically targeted intervention is unlikely to work,
because there is no barrier for an intervention to exploit.
Caveat on that inference. The argument runs from an absence of detectable structure to an assertion of free
gene flow. Low F is also consistent with recent common ancestry and with large effective population size
ST
holding drift down. Distinguishing "currently well connected" from "recently separated but not yet
differentiated" requires demographic inference these analyses have not attempted.

9.2 On the design limitation specifically
[Interpreted] Limitations 1 and 2 are not analytical shortcomings that better methods would overcome. They
are properties of the Pf8 metadata. No reanalysis of these data can produce a zone effect separable from a
region effect, because for three of the four zones there is only one region in each. This should be stated
plainly in any write-up rather than managed with hedging language, and it bears directly on how the thesis's
objectives can be framed.
[Proposed] If zone-level inference is essential to the thesis, the route is additional sampling with recorded
collection coordinates — ideally within the Sudan savanna, and within regions that straddle a zone boundary,
where zone and region could be decoupled. Whether that is feasible within the project is a question for the
author.

What is reproducible. The analysis runs end to end from a public data source. All inputs except the zone
polygon layer are retrievable from https://pf8-release.cog.sanger.ac.uk. Random seeds are fixed
(config.RANDOM_SEED = 42; ADMIXTURE -s 42; TreeMix -seed 42; BayPass -seed 42). Stage 3a is resumable

What is not. Three gaps. (i) The provenance of data/ghanaAEZ.geojson is undocumented, so the spatial join

10. Conclusion and current state of the research
10.1 What has been established
29. Ghanaian P. falciparum shows very low differentiation between agro-ecological zones (maximum
pairwise Hudson FST 0.0024, 95% CI 0.0014–0.0034). Five independent analyses agree. This is a well-
supported result.
30. Isolation by distance is detectable at administrative-region level (Mantel r = 0.400, p = 0.029) but not
at zone level, where the permutation test lacks resolution. Moderately supported.
31. No ancestry structure corresponds to geography. ADMIXTURE's preferred K = 2 improves on K = 1 by
0.5 per cent of cross-validation error, with near-uniform components across zones. Well supported as a
negative result.
32. A strictly bifurcating population tree is inadequate; one migration edge raises variance explained
from 0.876 to 0.935. Supported, though the specific edges are not identifiable with four populations.
33. Fifteen SNPs in 11 genes exceed a calibrated differentiation threshold, headed by AAT1
(PF3D7_0629500), a locus with an established connection to chloroquine-resistance evolution.
Provisional — a candidate list requiring independent support.
34. Mixed-infection frequency orders along the north–south gradient (0.597 in Guinea savanna to 0.444
in coastal savanna). Observed as a by-product of quality control, not analysed as a result in its own right.

10.4 Most valuable next steps
35. Run the Fws and MAF sensitivity analyses (Section 8.2, items 1 and 2). These are inexpensive, use
code already written, and address the two most likely reviewer objections.
36. Reframe the thesis objectives in light of the collinearity. The question "do ecological zones structure

falciparum spatially structured at all, and what does that imply for surveillance design?" can be, and has

negative result, because it tells the thesis which of its questions the available data can and cannot support —
and it does so before, rather than after, the marker-level analysis is built on top of them.

A. Explain it simply
Imagine you want to know whether malaria parasites in northern Ghana are a different population from
those on the coast. If they are separate, a drug-resistance mutation appearing in Accra might stay there for
years, and you would need to watch each region separately. If they are one population that happens to be
spread out, a mutation appearing anywhere will travel everywhere, and watching a few places is enough.
To find out, we took the genomes of Ghanaian parasites from a large public dataset, worked out which
ecological zone each one came from, and asked a series of questions with increasingly sophisticated tools. Do
parasites from different zones look genetically different? Do parasites further apart look more different than
parasites close together? Can a computer sort them into ancestry groups that match the zones? Is there any
gene in the genome that looks unusually different between zones, which might mean it is adapting to local
conditions?
The answers, in order: barely; a little, and only when we use administrative regions rather than zones; no;
and we found fifteen candidate positions in eleven genes, one of which — a gene called AAT1 — has a
published link to chloroquine resistance.
So the overall picture is of one parasite population spread across the country, mixing freely enough that
geography makes only a small difference. That is a useful answer. It means national surveillance does not
need to be spatially comprehensive to be representative — but it also means you could not contain an
outbreak of resistance by targeting one region.
There is an important catch, and it is about the data rather than the analysis. Each parasite's location is
recorded only as the administrative region it came from, not the actual collection site. And three of the four
ecological zones in this dataset contain exactly one region each. So "Guinea savanna" and "Upper East" are
the same group of samples under two different names. We cannot tell whether any difference we see is
caused by ecology or by something else about that region — its health system, its sampling protocol, or
simply the years in which it was sampled, which also differ between zones. This is not something a cleverer
analysis can fix.

40. The GQ filter removal (Section 4.2.5). This raised the SNP count by 57 per cent and so affects every
downstream result. The evidence that GQ was removing good data is indirect — high median depth
among failing calls — rather than a direct concordance check. Question: are you satisfied that
intermediate GQ in a haploid organism called as diploid reflects model ambiguity rather than genuine
uncertainty? If not, the whole analysis should be re-run at GQ ≥ 20 and the two compared.
41. Disabling the call-rate MAD rule (Section 4.2.3). Decided after seeing its effect on sample counts.
Question: would you have made the same decision if the counts had gone the other way? If not,
consider whether the absolute floor should instead be raised to 0.90 as a compromise.
