[Heading 1] Abstract

[Normal] Objective. To quantify spatial population structure, gene flow and candidate signatures of local selection in Plasmodium falciparum across Ghana's agro-ecological zones, using the MalariaGEN Pf8 release, and to establish whether zone-level differentiation is detectable at all before interpreting any locus-specific signal.

[Normal] Data. Pf8 contains 33,325 samples and 12,493,205 variant sites. Ghana contributes 6,653 samples, of which 4,905 pass the release's own quality control. Isolates were collected between 2009 and 2021 and are geolocated only to administrative level 1, giving seven distinct coordinates across seven regions. Spatial joining to a Ghana agro-ecological zone layer placed these in four zones: Guinea savanna (n = 3,605), deciduous forest (n = 815), coastal savanna (n = 435) and transitional zone (n = 50). The Sudan savanna and the wet-evergreen and moist-evergreen forest zones are unrepresented.

[Normal] Methods. Genotypes were obtained from the Pf8 Zarr store over HTTPS, restricted to Ghanaian samples, sites passing the release filter, biallelic single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) and the GT, AD, DP and GQ fields. Sample quality control applied a within-host fixation index (Fws) threshold of 0.95, a call-rate floor of 0.80, a heterozygosity outlier rule and clonal pruning. Mixed calls were resolved to the majority allele by read depth, and allele counts were taken as one allele per isolate. Analyses comprised three ordinations of the Pf8 genotype-distance matrix (UMAP, t-SNE, PCoA), pairwise Hudson FST with a block jackknife, ADMIXTURE at K = 1–8 with cross-validation, Mantel tests of isolation by distance at zone and region level, TreeMix with 0–5 migration edges, and a BayPass XtX genome scan calibrated against 100,000 pseudo-observed neutral SNPs.

[Normal] Principal findings. After all filters, 3,359 SNPs across 1,900 isolates entered the population analyses. Zone differentiation is very low: the largest pairwise FST is 0.0024 (95% CI 0.0014–0.0034) between coastal and Guinea savanna, and two of six pairs have confidence intervals spanning zero. Isolation by distance is supported at region level (Mantel r = 0.400, p = 0.029, 7 populations, 21 pairs) but not at zone level (r = 0.578, p = 0.090, 4 populations, 6 pairs), where only 24 distinct permutations exist. ADMIXTURE cross-validation error is minimised at K = 2 (0.83736) but the margin over K = 1 (0.84199) is small and ancestry proportions are nearly uniform across zones. TreeMix fit improves monotonically to a plateau at three migration edges, beyond the two that four populations can identify. The BayPass scan returns 15 outlier SNPs above a calibrated XtX threshold of 11.23, intersecting 11 genes including the amino-acid transporter AAT1 (PF3D7_0629500, two outlier SNPs, maximum XtX 24.44). No validated antimalarial resistance locus appears among the outliers.

[Normal] Current conclusion. The Ghanaian parasite population behaves as a largely panmictic unit at the resolution these data permit, with a weak distance-related signal that is detectable at region level. The analysis cannot currently separate ecological-zone effects from administrative-region effects, because three of the four zones are each filled by a single region; nor can it separate spatial from temporal effects, because sampling year is strongly confounded with zone. The outlier set is a provisional candidate list, not a demonstration of local adaptation. These results should be treated as the baseline characterisation that the marker-level thesis questions depend on, not as answers to them.

[Heading 1] 1. Introduction and background

[Heading 2] 1.1 The problem

[Normal] Malaria transmission in Ghana is spatially heterogeneous, and the heterogeneity tracks ecology. The country spans a south–north gradient of agro-ecological zones that differ in rainfall, vector species composition, seasonality and consequently in entomological inoculation rate. If parasite populations in these zones are demographically separate to any meaningful degree, then drug-resistance alleles, antigenic variants and locally adaptive alleles can differ in frequency between them, and a national surveillance programme built on a handful of sentinel sites will mis-estimate the national picture. If instead the zones exchange parasites freely, a resistance allele arising anywhere will spread everywhere, and the surveillance question becomes one of timing rather than geography.

[Normal] The two scenarios demand different control responses, and they are distinguishable from genomic data. The present analysis addresses the prior question that both depend on: how much spatial structure is there in Ghanaian P. falciparum, and is it enough to support zone-level inference at all?

[Normal] Scope. The thesis proposal and literature review (supplied separately as "genomic analysis of Consistent signatures of selection.docx", 145 paragraphs, approximately 11,400 words) set out the biological rationale, the resistance-marker panel and the published context. This report does not restate that material. It documents what was computed, on what data, with which decisions, and what the output actually shows.

[Heading 2] 1.2 Objectives addressed by the present analysis

[Normal] The thesis defines three specific objectives. The work documented here engages all three, but completes none of them. Their status is as follows.

[Heading 2] 1.3 Research questions as operationalised

[Normal] The thesis questions were translated into the following testable forms. The translation is itself an analytical decision and is revisited in Section 5.

[List Paragraph] Is between-zone differentiation distinguishable from zero? Operationalised as pairwise Hudson FST with block-jackknife confidence intervals.

[List Paragraph] Does genetic distance increase with geographic distance? Operationalised as a Mantel test of FST/(1 − FST) against log geographic distance, run at both zone and region level.

[List Paragraph] Do individual isolates assign to zone-associated ancestry components? Operationalised as ADMIXTURE at K = 1–8 with 10-fold cross-validation, and as visual inspection of three ordinations.

[List Paragraph] Is there evidence of gene flow beyond a simple bifurcating population tree? Operationalised as TreeMix with 0–5 migration edges.

[List Paragraph] Which loci are more differentiated than neutral demography predicts? Operationalised as a BayPass XtX scan with a threshold calibrated on pseudo-observed data simulated under the estimated population covariance matrix.

[Normal] [Interpreted] Questions 1–4 are answerable at the resolution these data permit and are answered, provisionally, in Section 6. Question 5 is answerable only in the weak sense of producing a ranked candidate list; with four populations and 3,359 SNPs the scan has limited power, and a negative result for any particular locus carries little information.

[Normal] [Observed] Pf8 geolocates isolates to administrative level 1. Individual-level geographic distance does not exist in these data, so no analysis can resolve structure finer than the region. [Heading 2] 3.2 Stage 1 — manifest filtering

[Normal] What and why. The manifest is restricted to Ghana and then to isolates flagged QC-pass by the release. Relying on the release's flag rather than re-deriving sample quality from raw data is a deliberate deference to MalariaGEN's own curation, which has access to sequencing-run level information this analysis does not.

[Normal] Implementation note. The script captures each retained isolate's row index in the full 33,325-sample distance matrix as the column pf8_row. This is the only safe way to slice the Ghanaian block later: the pandas index would shift under any subsequent filter, whereas pf8_row is fixed at the moment of extraction. Step 3 asserts that the maximum pf8_row does not exceed the matrix dimension, which would catch a Pf7/Pf8 mismatch.

[Normal] [Observed] 6,653 Ghanaian rows → 4,905 after the QC filter (1,748 dropped). pf8_row range 2,301–29,498.

[Heading 2] 3.3 Stage 2 — agro-ecological zone assignment

[Normal] What and why. Isolate coordinates are joined to the zone polygon layer with GeoPandas, giving each isolate a zone label. Because the coordinates are region centroids, the join is effectively a lookup table from seven points to four zones.

[Normal] Assumption. That a region's centroid is a reasonable proxy for where its isolates were collected. For compact regions this is tolerable; for regions that straddle a zone boundary it is not. The script prints a region × zone cross-tabulation specifically so that boundary-straddling regions can be checked by eye, and flags Brong Ahafo (forest/transition) and Volta (coastal/Guinea savanna) as the two warranting manual review.

[Normal] [Observed] All 4,905 isolates matched exactly one polygon; zero unmatched, zero duplicate matches. Resulting counts: Guinea savanna 3,605, deciduous forest 815, coastal savanna 435, transitional zone 50.

[Normal] [Proposed] The Brong Ahafo and Volta assignments have not been manually reviewed against the map. Volta in particular contributes 21 isolates to the deciduous forest zone, and its centroid sits near a boundary in the layer.

[Heading 2] 3.4 Stage 3 — three ordinations of the genotype distance matrix

[Normal] What and why. Pf8 publishes a precomputed mean genotype distance matrix over all 33,325 samples. The Ghanaian block (4,905 × 4,905) is sliced out by memory-mapping the 4.44 GB file and taking the submatrix, which keeps peak memory in the low hundreds of megabytes. Three dimensionality-reduction methods are then applied to the same matrix so that they can be compared on equal terms.

[List Paragraph] UMAP (n_neighbors = 30, min_dist = 0.1, metric = precomputed) — preserves local neighbourhood structure with partial global layout. These are the parameter values specified in the thesis workflow.

[List Paragraph] t-SNE (perplexity = 30, metric = precomputed, init = random) — preserves local neighbourhoods only. Initialisation must be random rather than PCA-based because the input is a distance matrix rather than feature vectors.

[List Paragraph] PCoA (classical metric multidimensional scaling) — Gower double-centring followed by an eigendecomposition. Unlike the other two it is linear, so axis scaling is interpretable and each axis carries a proportion of variation explained.

[Normal] Comparison metrics. Because the three methods optimise different objectives, a visual comparison alone would be uninformative. Three quantitative measures are computed on the same embeddings: trustworthiness (do points that are close in two dimensions correspond to genuine near neighbours in the full matrix?), the Spearman correlation between pairwise distances in the embedding and in the original matrix on a 2,000-sample subset (does the embedding preserve global structure?), and the silhouette coefficient by zone (do the zone labels separate?). The silhouette is also computed on the original distance matrix, which gives the ceiling that any embedding could achieve.

[Normal] Deviation from the thesis workflow. The workflow document specifies UMAP for this step. t-SNE and PCoA were added at the user's request during this session so that the three could be compared. This is an addition, not a substitution: the UMAP configuration is unchanged.

[Heading 2] 3.5 Stage 3a — genotype retrieval

[Normal] What and why. This stage did not exist in the supplied pipeline and was written during this session to replace a failed VCF download (Section 5.1). It fetches genotypes directly from the Pf8 Zarr store over HTTPS, chunk by chunk, retaining only what the downstream steps read.

[Normal] The Zarr store is chunked 131,072 variants × 100 samples per field. Because the 6,653 Ghanaian samples fall in 77 of the 334 sample-chunk columns, and the sites of interest in 95 of the 96 variant-chunk rows, only a subset of the grid must be transferred. Each chunk is downloaded, decompressed in memory, sliced to the Ghanaian columns and the retained rows, then written to a local store and discarded, so peak memory stays at a few gigabytes and the ~300 GB transferred is never written to disk.

[Normal] Sites are retained where FILTER_PASS is true, is_snp is true and numalt equals 1. Fields retained are GT, AD (reference and first alternate only), DP and GQ.

[Normal] [Observed] 2,190,026 of 12,493,205 Pf8 sites are PASS biallelic SNPs. Every one of them carries RegionType = "Core", confirmed by a direct tabulation. The transfer moved 299.8 GB in 3.20 hours at approximately 26 MB/s with zero failed requests, producing a 20.57 GB local store.

[Normal] Consequence for the core-genome filter. The supplied pipeline expects an optional BED file of core-genome intervals (data/pf_core_regions.bed), absent here, and warns that without it subtelomeric var/rifin regions will contaminate the outlier scan. The tabulation above resolves that concern empirically: because all PASS biallelic SNPs are annotated Core, the PASS filter already performs the exclusion the BED would have performed. The warning the pipeline prints at Step 4a is therefore a false alarm in this dataset.

[Normal] Validation. The store was verified against the one completely downloaded VCF (chromosome 10) and against two partially downloaded ones (chromosomes 1 and 13), in three independent 35–40 kb windows. In each window the site list matched exactly and all four genotype fields agreed value for value: 3,957, 3,470 and 4,378 PASS biallelic SNPs respectively, with zero differing values across 158 million, 139 million and 175 million compared values. The verification is in logs/step03a_fetch_callset.log.

[Heading 2] 3.6 Stage 3b — isolate quality control

[Normal] Four per-isolate checks are applied, computed from a panel of 50,000 biallelic PASS SNPs spread proportionally across all 14 contigs.

[Normal] Within-host fixation index (Fws). Fws measures how far an isolate's read-level allele frequencies depart from the population's. A clonal infection has read frequencies near 0 or 1 at every site and Fws near 1; a mixed infection carries intermediate frequencies and a lower value. It is computed here as

[Normal] Fws  =  1 − ( Σi Hw,i / Σi Hs,i ),   where Hw = 2q(1 − q) from the isolate's own read counts and Hs = 2p(1 − p) from pooled read counts across qualifying isolates.

[Normal] Sites contribute only where total depth is at least 5. The conventional monoclonality threshold of 0.95 is applied.

[Normal] Call rate. The proportion of panel SNPs with a genotype call. The pipeline applies an absolute floor and, optionally, a median-absolute-deviation outlier rule; the choice between them is discussed in Section 5.4.

[Normal] Heterozygosity outliers. Blood-stage P. falciparum is haploid, so a heterozygous call indicates a mixed infection or a genotyping error rather than true heterozygosity. Isolates more than three median absolute deviations above the median heterozygous call rate are flagged. This check is largely redundant with Fws and is retained as an independent cross-check.

[Normal] Clonal pruning. Near-identical isolates (outbreak-linked or repeat-sampled parasites) inflate within-zone homogeneity and therefore inflate between-zone FST. The cached Ghanaian distance submatrix from Stage 3 is thresholded, connected components are extracted with a single-linkage rule, and one representative is retained per component — chosen by best call rate, then best Fws, then identifier, so the choice is deterministic.

[Heading 2] 3.7 Stage 4a — building the genotype matrix

[Normal] This stage converts the Zarr store into the single filtered genotype matrix that every subsequent stage reads. The order of operations matters and is deliberate:

[List Paragraph] Site-level filters on the small one-dimensional arrays (FILTER_PASS, is_snp, numalt = 1), screening the whole genome before any genotype is fetched.

[List Paragraph] Genotype-level masking: calls below a read-depth floor are set to missing. A low-confidence call thus becomes missing data rather than a confident wrong answer, which matters because the missingness filter at step 5 then sees it for what it is.

[List Paragraph] Sample alignment: genotype columns are reordered to match the metadata table, so that sample order is fixed once and never re-derived.

[List Paragraph] Mixed-call resolution: heterozygous calls are replaced by the allele with more reads; ties become missing (Section 5.3).

[List Paragraph] Variant missingness filter (≤ 10 per cent), then sample missingness filter (≤ 20 per cent), then minor allele frequency filter (≥ 5 per cent). Missingness is applied before MAF so that a SNP is not retained on the strength of a handful of confident calls.

[Normal] Thresholds. MAF > 5 per cent and missingness < 10 per cent are specified in the thesis workflow document for Step 4 and were not altered. The depth floor and the sample-missingness bound are pipeline defaults; the depth and quality settings were revised during this session and that revision is documented in Section 5.5.

[Heading 2] 3.8 Stage 4b — pairwise differentiation

[Normal] Estimator. Hudson's FST is computed per locus and summed as a ratio of averages — numerator and denominator accumulated across loci before dividing — rather than as an average of per-locus ratios, which is upwardly biased. Hudson's estimator is preferred over Weir and Cockerham's here because it is insensitive to unequal sample sizes between populations, which is pertinent when one zone has 1,334 isolates and another has 25.

[Normal] Uncertainty. Standard errors come from a block jackknife over contiguous blocks of SNPs, which accounts for linkage between nearby sites. Blocks are 2,000 SNPs by default, reduced automatically to at most one twentieth of the available SNPs.

[Normal] Ploidy handling. Allele counts are taken as one allele per isolate (Section 5.3). This matters: counting both copies of a diploid-coded haploid genotype would double every sample size entering the FST correction, the TreeMix drift standard errors and the BayPass XtX statistic, making all three anticonservative.

[Heading 2] 3.9 Stages 5 to 9

[Normal] ADMIXTURE (Stage 5). Genotypes are exported to PLINK transposed text format, converted to binary, LD-pruned (50-SNP window, 10-SNP step, r² threshold 0.2) and run through ADMIXTURE for K = 2 to 8 with 10-fold cross-validation. Pruning is not optional: linked SNPs carry redundant information and inflate apparent structure. K = 1 was run separately during this session to provide the no-structure reference point that the supplied sweep omits.

[Normal] Mantel test (Stage 6). Isolation by distance is tested by regressing linearised differentiation, FST / (1 − FST), on the logarithm of great-circle distance computed on the WGS84 ellipsoid. Significance comes from a permutation test on the distance matrices (999 permutations, one-sided, since isolation by distance predicts a positive association). The test is run at two levels: four zones, and seven administrative regions.

[Normal] TreeMix (Stage 7). A maximum-likelihood population graph is fitted with 0 to 5 migration edges, rooted on Guinea savanna, using a jackknife block size of 50 SNPs. Model fit is summarised as the proportion of variance in between-population covariance explained, computed over off-diagonal entries only, matching TreeMix's own definition.

[Normal] BayPass (Stage 8). The core model estimates the population covariance matrix Ω from genome-wide allele counts, then reports XtX per SNP — a differentiation statistic standardised by that covariance structure. A SNP is called an outlier when its XtX exceeds a threshold taken from pseudo-observed data simulated under the estimated Ω. The logic is that a threshold drawn from the empirical XtX distribution assumes a fixed proportion of the genome is under selection, whereas a threshold from simulated neutral data assumes only that the demographic model is adequate.

[Normal] Annotation (Stage 9). Outlier positions are intersected with protein-coding gene features from PlasmoDB-71 using bedtools, and cross-referenced against a panel of eight validated antimalarial resistance loci and against antigenic and invasion gene families.

[Heading 2] 4.2 Rationale, origin and consequences of each non-trivial filter

[Heading 3] 4.2.1 Pf8 QC-pass flag (filter 2)

[Normal] Purpose and origin. Pre-specified. Removes isolates MalariaGEN itself judged unreliable, on the basis of sequencing-run information not available downstream.

[Normal] Effect. 1,748 of 6,653 isolates removed (26.3 per cent). The loss is not uniform across regions: Upper East loses 1,216 (25.2 per cent) and Central loses 377 (55.0 per cent), while Volta loses none.

[Normal] [Hypothesized] Differential loss by region most likely reflects differences in sample collection, storage or sequencing batch rather than anything biological, but the manifest does not carry the fields needed to confirm this.

[Heading 3] 4.2.2 Fws ≥ 0.95 (filter 8)

[Normal] Purpose. To exclude polyclonal infections. A mixed infection contributes allele frequencies from several parasite genomes to a single genotype column; ADMIXTURE reads that as intermediate ancestry, so an uncorrected analysis risks describing the transmission-intensity gradient rather than the ancestry structure it is meant to estimate.

[Normal] Origin. Pre-specified. 0.95 is the conventional cut and is the pipeline default (config.MIN_FWS). It was re-confirmed during this session against the observed Fws distribution, which shows a pronounced mode above 0.95 and a long left shoulder (Figure 6.2, third panel), so the threshold falls at a natural break rather than cutting through a dense region.

[Normal] Effect and its most important consequence. 2,810 of 4,905 isolates removed — by far the largest single exclusion. Critically, the polyclonal fraction varies systematically by zone: Guinea savanna 0.597, deciduous forest 0.539, transitional zone 0.500, coastal savanna 0.444.

[Normal] [Interpreted] This gradient is itself a transmission signal — higher transmission produces more mixed infections — so the filter removes a greater share of isolates from the high-transmission north than from the lower-transmission coast. The retained sample is therefore not a random subsample of the Ghanaian parasite population, and the degree of non-randomness differs by zone. Because the filter targets monoclonality rather than any allele, the direct effect on allele frequencies should be modest, but this has not been tested.

[Normal] [Proposed] Re-run the FST and ADMIXTURE analyses at Fws ≥ 0.90 and ≥ 0.80 (projected to retain 2,341 and 2,924 isolates respectively) and compare. If conclusions are unchanged, the sensitivity concern is discharged cheaply.

[Heading 3] 4.2.3 Call-rate outlier rule (filter 6) — disabled

[Normal] What the pipeline did. A median-absolute-deviation rule flags isolates whose call rate falls more than three MADs below the median. Because Ghanaian call rates are tightly bunched near 1 (median 0.9962), the resulting bound sits at 0.982 and flagged 1,281 isolates, of which 449 failed no other check.

[Normal] Decision and origin. Session decision, taken on exploratory output. The rule was disabled, leaving only the absolute floor of 0.80 (config.MIN_SAMPLE_CALL_RATE, pre-specified). The reasoning: the MAD-flagged, otherwise-passing isolates had a median call rate of 0.956, which is not poor data in absolute terms, and Step 4a applies an independent sample-missingness bound later in any case. A MAD rule is designed to find outliers in a dispersed distribution; applied to a tight one it becomes an arbitrary strict threshold.

[Normal] Effect. Retaining these isolates raised the QC-passing count from 1,566 to 2,017 and the final analysis set from 1,862 to 1,900.

[Normal] Honesty note. This threshold was chosen after seeing its effect on sample counts, not in advance. The justification above is a post-hoc rationalisation of a decision made during exploratory analysis. It is defensible, but it was not pre-registered, and a reviewer is entitled to ask whether the alternative was examined with equal care. Table 4.2 records the alternatives that were quantified before the choice was made.

[Heading 3] 4.2.5 Genotype quality mask (filter 10)

[Normal] Problem. The pipeline default masked calls below DP 5 or GQ 20. Under that default the final matrix held 2,143 SNPs. Removing the GQ component raised it to 3,359.

[Normal] Evidence gathered before deciding. A 40,000-SNP diagnostic panel showed that 12.6 per cent of homozygous-reference and 12.4 per cent of homozygous-alternate calls fall below GQ 20, with median depths of 46 and 40 respectively — that is, the GQ < 20 population is not a low-depth population. Restricted to calls with DP ≥ 10, only 3.0 per cent of homozygous-reference and 0.7 per cent of homozygous-alternate calls still fail GQ 20. Applying the GQ rule halved the number of common SNPs surviving the missingness bar (from 51.0 per cent of panel SNPs under DP-only masking to... in fact missingness ≤ 10 per cent fell from 74.3 per cent of SNPs under DP-only to 51.0 per cent under DP+GQ), while mean alternate-allele frequency at common SNPs moved only from 0.282 to 0.294.

[Normal] Decision and origin. Session decision, taken on that evidence and confirmed with the user. config.MIN_GENOTYPE_GQ was set to 0, retaining DP ≥ 5. The reasoning: the GQ rule was removing a large number of common SNPs at substantial cost to statistical power, while barely changing allele frequencies — that is, it was costing precision without buying accuracy.

[Normal] [Interpreted] The likely explanation is that GQ is a Phred-scaled confidence in the called genotype relative to alternatives; in a haploid organism forced into a diploid calling model, intermediate GQ values can arise from the model's own ambiguity rather than from poor data. This interpretation is consistent with the depth evidence but was not independently verified.

[Heading 3] 4.2.6 MAF ≥ 5 per cent (filter 14)

[Normal] Origin. Pre-specified in the thesis workflow ("MAF > 5%") and not altered.

[Normal] Effect — the most consequential number in the analysis. 1,634,808 SNPs pass the missingness filter; 3,359 pass MAF. This is a reduction of 99.79 per cent.

[Normal] [Observed] The diagnostic panel establishes that this is a property of the organism and the population, not of the pipeline: with no genotype masking whatsoever, only about 4,832 SNPs genome-wide reach MAF ≥ 5 per cent with missingness ≤ 10 per cent, and about 5,529 reach MAF ≥ 5 per cent ignoring missingness entirely. The ceiling is therefore roughly 5,500 SNPs, and the analysis retains 3,359 of them.

[Normal] [Interpreted] P. falciparum carries an extremely large number of rare variants and comparatively few common ones within a single country. A 5 per cent MAF filter in this setting is far more aggressive than the same nominal threshold would be in, say, a human cohort. Every downstream analysis — FST, ADMIXTURE, TreeMix, BayPass — operates on these 3,359 sites, and the BayPass scan in particular has limited power at this density.

[Normal] [Proposed] Repeat the main analyses at MAF ≥ 1 per cent (projected roughly 8,500–12,400 SNPs) as a sensitivity check. This departs from the thesis specification and so should be reported as a robustness analysis rather than as the primary result, but the gain in power for the outlier scan could be substantial.

[Heading 2] 4.3 Selection bias summary

[Normal] Three biases are introduced or amplified by the filtering, in decreasing order of concern.

[List Paragraph] Transmission-intensity bias (from the Fws filter). The retained sample under-represents high-transmission settings, and does so unevenly across zones. This is the bias most likely to affect between-zone comparisons.

[List Paragraph] Regional bias (from the Pf8 QC flag and from the sampling frame itself). Upper East dominates every stage. No filter corrects this and none was intended to.

[List Paragraph] Allele-frequency bias (from the MAF filter). The analysis sees only the common end of the frequency spectrum. Rare variants, which is where most recent selection signal would sit, are invisible to it.

[Normal] [Interpreted] None of these is an error. Each follows from a defensible choice. But together they mean the final 1,900-isolate, 3,359-SNP dataset is a particular view of Ghanaian P. falciparum — monoclonal infections, common variants, Upper East–heavy — and conclusions should be stated as applying to that view.

[Heading 1] 5. Analytical decisions and iterations

[Normal] This section reconstructs the decisions taken during the analysis. Where a decision was put to the user and answered, that is stated. Where the rationale is reconstructed after the fact, it is labelled as such.

[Heading 2] 5.1 Replacing the VCF route with direct Zarr retrieval

[Normal] Problem. The project had 14 per-chromosome VCFs in data/, produced by data/extract_ghana.sh, which streamed each Pf8 chromosome VCF from Sanger through bcftools view with a Ghana sample list. Thirteen of the fourteen were truncated. Inspection of the BGZF end-of-file marker and decoding of the last record in each file showed coverage from 9 per cent (chromosome 12) to 53 per cent (chromosome 9); only chromosome 10 was complete.

[Normal] Cause. bcftools aborted with "[E::bcf_calc_ac] Incorrect AN/AC counts" while recomputing allele counts for the sample subset. Each truncation point matches its recorded error position exactly — chromosome 12 failed at position 200,583 and its last record is at 200,581. The extraction script echoed "Finished" regardless of the exit status, so the failures went unnoticed. The errors are preserved in data/extract_ghana.log and data/chr05_extract.log; the consequent end-of-file warnings are in data/index_tbi.log.

[Normal] Constraint. The one complete VCF, chromosome 10, is 34.7 GB for 1.69 Mb of sequence. At that rate the full 23.3 Mb genome would require approximately 480 GB, against 352 GB free on the data volume. A simple re-download was therefore not possible.

[Normal] Options put to the user. 

[TABLE]
Option | Transfer | Local disk | Time | Script changes
A. Pf8 Zarr store, Ghana samples and PASS biallelic SNPs only (chosen) | ~300 GB | ~25–40 GB | ~3–4 h | One new script
B. Resume the VCFs with bcftools -I, slimmed to needed fields | ~1.6 TB | moderate | 1–2 days | None, plus slow VCF→Zarr conversion
C. Re-download all VCFs from scratch, slimmed | ~2.2 TB | moderate | longest | None
[/TABLE]

[Normal] Table 5.1. Genotype-retrieval options presented. Estimates for A derive from a direct probe of the Zarr store's chunk grid; B and C are extrapolated from the observed chromosome-10 download.

[Normal] Decision. Option A, chosen by the user. A fourth implicit option — the project's own step04a_prepare_callset_malariagen.py, which pulls from Google Cloud via the malariagen_data package — was not pursued because it would have required changes to run_all.sh and because Step 3b reads the local store directly.

[Normal] Consequence and verification. The new script produced a 20.57 GB store verified exactly against three VCF windows (Section 3.5). On that evidence, and with the user's approval, the 13 truncated VCFs and their indexes were deleted, freeing about 125 GB; the complete chromosome-10 VCF was retained as a verification reference.

[Normal] [Interpreted] Beyond solving the immediate problem, this route is strictly better for the analysis: it removes the AC/AN recomputation that caused the failure, it carries the RegionType annotation that makes the missing core-genome BED unnecessary, and it is reproducible from a public URL rather than from a 160 GB local artefact.

[Heading 2] 5.2 Adding t-SNE and PCoA alongside UMAP

[Normal] Problem and decision. The thesis workflow specifies UMAP. The user asked for alternative ordinations so that the three could be compared. Both were added to Stage 3, reading the same distance matrix, with a quantitative comparison table.

[Normal] Why it mattered more than expected. UMAP and t-SNE are non-linear and their inter-cluster distances carry no quantitative meaning, a caveat the original script already printed. PCoA is linear, so its axes are interpretable and carry explicit variance proportions. With structure as weak as it is here, the PCoA result — 1.3 per cent of variation on the first two axes — is considerably more informative than any visual impression of the UMAP plot, and would not have been available from the specified workflow alone.

[Heading 2] 5.3 Ploidy: how to count alleles in a haploid parasite coded as diploid

[Normal] Problem. Blood-stage P. falciparum is haploid, but Pf8 records genotypes in diploid form: 0/0 or 1/1 for a clonal call, 0/1 where reads support both alleles (a mixed infection). The supplied step04b_fst.py called scikit-allel's count_alleles on the diploid array, which counts two gene copies per isolate. The same cached counts feed TreeMix and BayPass.

[Normal] Why this is a defect, not a convention. Hudson's FST carries a sample-size correction; TreeMix derives drift standard errors from counts; BayPass's XtX is standardised by them. Doubling every sample size makes all three anticonservative — narrower intervals, more apparently significant edges, more apparent outliers — without changing the point estimates much.

[Normal] Options put to the user. 

[List Paragraph] Haploid, mixed calls to missing — use the pipeline's existing --het-to-missing switch, then count one allele per isolate. Simplest; discards the mixed calls entirely.

[List Paragraph] Haploid, mixed calls to the majority allele (chosen) — assign the allele with more reads, ties to missing, then count one allele per isolate. Retains the information in the read depths.

[List Paragraph] Leave as written — point estimates barely move, but uncertainty stays understated throughout.

[Normal] Implementation and verification. A function resolve_mixed_calls was added to step04a_prepare_callset.py, reading AD a block at a time only for SNPs where some retained isolate carries a mixed call. A companion isolate_allele_counts was added to callset_io.py; it takes the first gene copy once mixed calls are resolved, and falls back to diploid counting with an explicit warning if any mixed call remains. Both were verified against a hand-computed synthetic case (3 SNPs × 4 isolates, including a tie and a missing genotype) before being applied to real data.

[Normal] [Observed] On the real dataset, 194,167 mixed calls were resolved: 129,665 to reference, 55,582 to alternate, and 8,920 tied and set to missing. The residual heterozygous call rate printed by Step 4a is 0.0000 to four decimal places, consistent with the Fws filter having already removed most mixed infections.

[Normal] [Interpreted] The asymmetry — 2.3 reference calls for every alternate call — is expected, since at a site where most isolates carry the reference allele, a mixed infection is more likely to be reference-dominant. It is not evidence of a reference bias in the resolution rule itself, though that possibility has not been formally excluded.

[Heading 2] 5.4 Sample QC thresholds

[Normal] Three thresholds were put to the user after the first QC run, each accompanied by the counts each option would produce. The call-rate and clonality decisions are discussed in Sections 4.2.3 and 4.2.4. The Fws threshold was retained at the conventional 0.95 (options at 0.90 and 0.80 were offered and declined).

[Normal] [Interpreted] Presenting the sample counts alongside each option made the trade-off explicit, but it also means these thresholds were selected with knowledge of their effect on sample size. That is standard exploratory practice and is reported here rather than concealed.

[Heading 2] 5.5 TreeMix jackknife block size

[Normal] Problem. The supplied configuration used 500 SNPs per jackknife block. With 3,359 SNPs that yields 6 or 7 blocks, far too few for stable standard errors, which in turn makes the migration-edge tests unreliable.

[Normal] Decision. Reduced to 50 SNPs, giving approximately 67 blocks. With retained SNPs averaging roughly 7 kb apart, a 50-SNP block spans about 350 kb, which comfortably exceeds the scale over which linkage disequilibrium persists in P. falciparum. The block therefore remains large enough to absorb linkage while being small enough to give a usable number of replicates. A 100-SNP alternative (about 34 blocks) was offered and declined.

[Heading 2] 5.6 BayPass calibration

[Normal] Problems found. Three, in the supplied step08b_baypass.sh.

[List Paragraph] The pseudo-observed data simulation passed the .geno matrix directly as sample.size. That file interleaves reference and alternate counts, giving 2 × npop columns, whereas simulate.baypass requires npop columns of total haploid sample size per SNP. The call would have failed, and the script would have fallen through to an empirical quantile.

[List Paragraph] Only 1,000 pseudo-observed SNPs were simulated. The threshold is the 0.999 quantile of their XtX, so it would have rested on a single simulated value.

[List Paragraph] The simulation applied no minor-allele-frequency filter, while the observed SNPs had been filtered at MAF ≥ 5 per cent. The resulting neutral distribution was not comparable to the observed one: 17.0 per cent of simulated sites had overall allele frequency below 0.05, against 0.5 per cent of observed sites.

[Normal] Corrections. Reference and alternate columns are now summed to total counts; the simulation uses 100,000 SNPs; and pi.maf is set to 0.05 to match the observed filter. A stale G.pod file is removed before each run, and an R failure now falls through to the documented empirical fallback instead of aborting the stage.

[Normal] [Observed] The correction moved the threshold materially: from 8.375 with 1,000 unfiltered simulated SNPs (40 outliers, 1.19 per cent of the scan) to 11.232 with 100,000 MAF-matched ones (15 outliers, 0.45 per cent). The reported results use the corrected calibration.

[Normal] [Interpreted] The direction is as expected: an unfiltered simulation contains rare sites whose XtX is low, which depresses the upper quantile and admits too many outliers. The 25 SNPs that fall between the two thresholds should be regarded as having failed calibration, not as marginal.

[Heading 2] 5.8 Decisions that may warrant reconsideration

[List Paragraph] Disabling the call-rate MAD rule (Section 4.2.3) was chosen on sample-count grounds after seeing the effect. A stricter absolute floor of 0.90 was offered and not taken; the data to compare it properly were not generated.

[List Paragraph] Retaining m = 3 as a candidate TreeMix model, when four populations support at most two identifiable migration edges, keeps a model in play that the data cannot really support. Both picks are reported deliberately, but the choice between them has not been made.

[List Paragraph] The MAF ≥ 5 per cent filter is faithful to the thesis specification but costs roughly 99.8 per cent of the sites that survive missingness filtering, and this was not re-examined after the SNP count became known.

[List Paragraph] Zone assignment for Brong Ahafo and Volta was never manually reviewed against the map, as Step 2 recommended.

[Normal] [Interpreted] An FST of 0.0024 means roughly 0.24 per cent of genetic variation is attributable to zone membership. For comparison, published within-country FST for P. falciparum routinely falls below 0.05, and continental-scale African subpopulation structure is itself modest (Amambua-Ngwa et al., 2019). The Ghanaian values sit an order of magnitude below even that within-country expectation. The practical reading is that these zones are not demographically separate populations; they are one population sampled in four places. The ranking of pairs is informative — and it is consistent with distance — but no threshold of biological significance is crossed.

[Normal] Statistical versus practical significance. [Interpreted] Three pairs are statistically distinguishable from zero, which with 1,900 isolates and 3,359 SNPs is a low bar. None is practically significant in the sense of implying restricted gene flow. A resistance allele arising in Accra would be expected to reach Bolgatanga; the question these data cannot answer is how quickly.

[Heading 2] 6.5 Isolation by distance

[Normal] [Observed] At region level (7 populations, 21 pairs, distances 87–578 km) the Mantel correlation is r = 0.400 with p = 0.029 against 999 permutations. At zone level (4 populations, 6 pairs, distances 145–557 km) it is r = 0.578 with p = 0.090.

[Normal] Figure 6.4. Isolation by distance at administrative-region level: linearised differentiation FST/(1 − FST) against log great-circle distance, 21 region pairs, with the least-squares line. Mantel r = 0.400, p = 0.029 (999 permutations, one-sided). Generated by scripts/step06_mantel.py as figures/mantel_ibd_region.png.

[Normal] Why the smaller correlation is the stronger result. The zone-level test has the larger correlation but the larger p-value, which is a power effect, not a contradiction. With four populations only 4! = 24 distinct orderings exist, so the finest attainable p-value is 0.042 and significance requires essentially the single best permutation. With seven regions there are 5,040 orderings and the test can resolve p down to 0.001. The region-level test is therefore the one to rely on, and it supports isolation by distance.

[Normal] [Interpreted] Taken with Section 6.4, the picture is of a continuously distributed population in which geographic distance has a weak but detectable effect on differentiation — the classic isolation-by-distance signature — rather than of discrete populations separated by barriers. Note that this says nothing about ecological zones as such: distance alone accounts for the pattern, and nothing in these data requires an ecological explanation.

[Normal] Caveat on the region-level test. [Interpreted] The region-level FST values are computed from the same 3,359 SNPs but with populations defined by administrative region. Volta contributes 21 isolates and Brong Ahafo 25, so several of the 21 pairs rest on small samples; five pairs in Figure 6.4 sit at or very near zero differentiation. The scatter is wide and the fitted relationship is driven substantially by the Upper East comparisons at the right-hand side of the plot.

A locus under strong but spatially uniform selection — which is what a nationally deployed drug produces — generates no XtX signal at all. The absence of pfcrt and pfk13 from this list is therefore not evidence that they are not under selection in Ghana; it is, if anything, weakly consistent with their being under uniform selection. Testing that requires a different analysis (Section 8.2).

[Heading 1] 7. Interpretation and discussion

[Heading 2] 7.1 What the analysis establishes

[Normal] Five independent lines of evidence converge on the same conclusion.

[TABLE]
Analysis | Result | Reading
Pairwise FST | Maximum 0.0024 | Zones share essentially all their genetic variation
Ordination (3 methods) | Silhouette by zone −0.016 on the raw distance matrix; PCoA PC1+PC2 = 1.3% | Zone labels do not describe the variation
ADMIXTURE | K = 2 minimises CV but improves on K = 1 by 0.5%; components near-uniform across zones | No ancestry structure aligned to geography
TreeMix | Bifurcating tree explains 87.6%; one edge raises it to 93.5% | Exchange between groups, not a branching history
Mantel | Region level r = 0.400, p = 0.029 | A weak but real distance effect
[/TABLE]

[Normal] Table 7.1. Convergent evidence on population structure.

[Normal] [Interpreted] Ghanaian P. falciparum behaves as a single, largely panmictic population with a weak isolation-by-distance gradient. This is a substantive answer to the thesis's first objective, and a negative one for the strong form of its hypothesis: the ecological zones are not acting as demographic barriers at a scale these data can detect.

[Normal] [Interpreted] This is not a surprising result, and that is a point in its favour. Ghana is approximately 670 km north to south, human mobility along the north–south corridor is substantial, P. falciparum undergoes obligate sexual recombination in the mosquito, and transmission across most of the country is high. All four favour genetic homogenisation. The finding is consistent with the continental picture, in which West African parasite populations form a single major subpopulation (Amambua-Ngwa et al., 2019), and with the observation that differentiation within West African countries is weak relative to the differentiation between endemicity strata that Mobegi et al. (2014) reported between The Gambia and Guinea.

[Heading 2] 7.2 What the analysis cannot establish, and why

[Normal] Zone effects cannot be separated from region effects. Three of four zones are each filled by exactly one administrative region (Table 2.2). For those three, "zone" and "region" are the same variable. Any difference attributed to ecology could equally be attributed to the region — its health system, its drug supply, its sampling protocol, its collection year. No statistical adjustment can separate variables that are collinear by construction.

[Normal] Spatial effects cannot be separated from temporal ones. Median collection year differs by six years across zones (Table 2.3), and for the transitional zone and coastal savanna the year ranges barely overlap. Over 2009–2021 Ghana changed first-line therapy and scaled up chemoprevention, so drug-related allele frequencies would be expected to shift with time regardless of geography.

[Normal] Barrier hypotheses cannot be tested as such. Testing whether a specific feature (the forest–savanna transition, the Volta Lake system) impedes parasite dispersal requires differentiation to be measurable across the feature and compared against differentiation at the same distance elsewhere. With four populations, six pairwise comparisons and FST values under 0.0024, there is no resolution to work with.

[Normal] The outlier list is a candidate list, not a finding. Fifteen SNPs is a small set from a small scan. No multiple-testing correction beyond the calibrated threshold has been applied, no replication is possible within these data, and nothing about an elevated XtX value establishes that a locus is under selection rather than drifting unusually or sitting near an assembly or alignment artefact.

[Heading 2] 7.3 Unexpected observations

[Normal] The MAF ceiling. That the whole Ghanaian genome yields only about 5,500 common SNPs was not anticipated and materially constrains every downstream analysis. It is a property of P. falciparum population genomics within a single country — an abundance of rare variation, comparatively little common variation — rather than a defect. But it means that a study of this design has considerably less power than the sample size of 1,900 isolates would suggest.

[Normal] Polyclonality as the clearest ecological signal. The one variable that orders cleanly along the north–south gradient is the fraction of mixed infections (0.597 in Guinea savanna to 0.444 in coastal savanna). This is a transmission-intensity signal, and it is arguably more informative about ecology than anything in the population-genetic results.

[Normal] [Hypothesized] If polyclonality tracks transmission intensity and transmission intensity tracks ecological zone, then Fws is itself a candidate zone-level phenotype worth analysing in its own right rather than used only as a QC filter. That analysis has not been performed.

[Normal] Likelihood monotonicity in TreeMix. The log-likelihood rises with every added migration edge, which is expected but means likelihood cannot select a model. The supplied script's selection rule would not have caught this; the defect is documented in Table 5.2.

[Heading 2] 7.4 Practical significance

[Normal] [Interpreted] For malaria control the operational implication of near-panmixia is clear and, in its way, useful. If Ghanaian parasites form one population, then a resistance allele established anywhere will spread nationally, and sentinel surveillance at a small number of sites is an adequate design for detecting emergence — the samples do not need to be spatially comprehensive to be representative. The corollary is less comfortable: containment of an emerging resistance focus by geographically targeted intervention is unlikely to work, because there is no barrier for an intervention to exploit.

[Normal] Caveat on that inference. The argument runs from an absence of detectable structure to an assertion of free gene flow. Low FST is also consistent with recent common ancestry and with large effective population size holding drift down. Distinguishing "currently well connected" from "recently separated but not yet differentiated" requires demographic inference these analyses have not attempted.

[Heading 2] 8.2 Checks proposed but not performed

[Normal] [Proposed] None of the following has been run. They are listed in order of expected value.

[List Paragraph] Fws sensitivity. Re-run FST, ADMIXTURE and the outlier scan at Fws ≥ 0.90 and ≥ 0.80 (projected 2,341 and 2,924 isolates). This is the single largest exclusion in the pipeline and the one with a documented zone-dependent bias; if conclusions are stable across these thresholds, the most serious filtering objection is answered.

[List Paragraph] MAF sensitivity. Repeat the main analyses at MAF ≥ 1 per cent (projected 8,500–12,400 SNPs). This would roughly triple the sites available to the outlier scan. Report as a robustness analysis, since it departs from the thesis specification.

[List Paragraph] Temporal control. Restrict to a single collection year or a narrow window, where sample sizes permit, and recompute FST. 2018 contributes 773 isolates across three zones and is the only year that could support this.

[List Paragraph] Region-level ADMIXTURE and TreeMix. Running these with populations defined by administrative region rather than zone would at least show whether the choice of grouping affects the conclusions, even though it cannot resolve the underlying collinearity.

[List Paragraph] Permutation test for the outlier set. Compare the functional composition of the 11 outlier genes against random gene sets matched for length and SNP density, to test whether the apparent enrichment for exported and host-interaction proteins exceeds chance.

[List Paragraph] Re-run Stage 3 ordinations on the QC-passing set. Stage 3 currently operates on the 4,905 pre-QC isolates, so the published ordinations include the polyclonal and clonally redundant isolates that every later stage excludes. Re-running on the 1,900-isolate set would make the figures consistent with the rest of the analysis, and might sharpen the structure.

[List Paragraph] Effective population size and divergence-time estimation, to distinguish ongoing gene flow from recent common ancestry (Section 7.4).

[List Paragraph] Manual review of the Brong Ahafo and Volta zone assignments against the polygon layer, as Step 2 recommends.

[Heading 2] 9.2 On the design limitation specifically

[Normal] [Interpreted] Limitations 1 and 2 are not analytical shortcomings that better methods would overcome. They are properties of the Pf8 metadata. No reanalysis of these data can produce a zone effect separable from a region effect, because for three of the four zones there is only one region in each. This should be stated plainly in any write-up rather than managed with hedging language, and it bears directly on how the thesis's objectives can be framed.

[Normal] [Proposed] If zone-level inference is essential to the thesis, the route is additional sampling with recorded collection coordinates — ideally within the Sudan savanna, and within regions that straddle a zone boundary, where zone and region could be decoupled. Whether that is feasible within the project is a question for the author.

[Heading 2] 9.3 Reproducibility status

[Normal] What is reproducible. The analysis runs end to end from a public data source. All inputs except the zone polygon layer are retrievable from https://pf8-release.cog.sanger.ac.uk. Random seeds are fixed (config.RANDOM_SEED = 42; ADMIXTURE -s 42; TreeMix -seed 42; BayPass -seed 42). Stage 3a is resumable and verifies its own output. The stage sequence is reproduced by: 

[Normal] bash scripts/run_all.sh --from 4 --parallel

[Normal] What is not. Three gaps. (i) The provenance of data/ghanaAEZ.geojson is undocumented, so the spatial join cannot be reproduced by a third party from public sources alone. (ii) The project is not under version control, so the exact script state at run time is not recoverable other than from the files currently on disk; the unmodified originals are in pf8_ghana_pipeline.zip. (iii) UMAP with a fixed random_state forces single-threaded execution and is deterministic, but t-SNE and the ADMIXTURE runs have not been verified to be bitwise reproducible across different thread counts.

[Normal] [Unknown] Whether the two numerically intensive stages (ADMIXTURE, BayPass) return identical results under a different thread count has not been tested.

[Heading 1] 10. Conclusion and current state of the research

[Heading 2] 10.1 What has been established

[List Paragraph] Ghanaian P. falciparum shows very low differentiation between agro-ecological zones (maximum pairwise Hudson FST 0.0024, 95% CI 0.0014–0.0034). Five independent analyses agree. This is a well-supported result.

[List Paragraph] Isolation by distance is detectable at administrative-region level (Mantel r = 0.400, p = 0.029) but not at zone level, where the permutation test lacks resolution. Moderately supported.

[List Paragraph] No ancestry structure corresponds to geography. ADMIXTURE's preferred K = 2 improves on K = 1 by 0.5 per cent of cross-validation error, with near-uniform components across zones. Well supported as a negative result.

[List Paragraph] A strictly bifurcating population tree is inadequate; one migration edge raises variance explained from 0.876 to 0.935. Supported, though the specific edges are not identifiable with four populations.

[List Paragraph] Fifteen SNPs in 11 genes exceed a calibrated differentiation threshold, headed by AAT1 (PF3D7_0629500), a locus with an established connection to chloroquine-resistance evolution. Provisional — a candidate list requiring independent support.

[List Paragraph] Mixed-infection frequency orders along the north–south gradient (0.597 in Guinea savanna to 0.444 in coastal savanna). Observed as a by-product of quality control, not analysed as a result in its own right.

[Heading 2] A. Explain it simply

[Normal] Imagine you want to know whether malaria parasites in northern Ghana are a different population from those on the coast. If they are separate, a drug-resistance mutation appearing in Accra might stay there for years, and you would need to watch each region separately. If they are one population that happens to be spread out, a mutation appearing anywhere will travel everywhere, and watching a few places is enough.

[Normal] To find out, we took the genomes of Ghanaian parasites from a large public dataset, worked out which ecological zone each one came from, and asked a series of questions with increasingly sophisticated tools. Do parasites from different zones look genetically different? Do parasites further apart look more different than parasites close together? Can a computer sort them into ancestry groups that match the zones? Is there any gene in the genome that looks unusually different between zones, which might mean it is adapting to local conditions?

[Normal] The answers, in order: barely; a little, and only when we use administrative regions rather than zones; no; and we found fifteen candidate positions in eleven genes, one of which — a gene called AAT1 — has a published link to chloroquine resistance.

[Normal] So the overall picture is of one parasite population spread across the country, mixing freely enough that geography makes only a small difference. That is a useful answer. It means national surveillance does not need to be spatially comprehensive to be representative — but it also means you could not contain an outbreak of resistance by targeting one region.

[Normal] There is an important catch, and it is about the data rather than the analysis. Each parasite's location is recorded only as the administrative region it came from, not the actual collection site. And three of the four ecological zones in this dataset contain exactly one region each. So "Guinea savanna" and "Upper East" are the same group of samples under two different names. We cannot tell whether any difference we see is caused by ecology or by something else about that region — its health system, its sampling protocol, or simply the years in which it was sampled, which also differ between zones. This is not something a cleverer analysis can fix.

[Heading 2] B. Methodological rationale

[Normal] For each major choice: what it does, why it may be appropriate, the alternatives, the assumptions it introduces, and what evidence would justify changing it.

[TABLE]
Choice | What it accomplishes / why appropriate | Alternatives | Assumptions introduced | What would justify changing it
Hudson's FST, ratio of averages | Gives a differentiation estimate insensitive to unequal sample sizes — important when zones range from 25 to 1,334 isolates. [Heading 2] C. Decisions requiring your review

[List Paragraph] The GQ filter removal (Section 4.2.5). This raised the SNP count by 57 per cent and so affects every downstream result. The evidence that GQ was removing good data is indirect — high median depth among failing calls — rather than a direct concordance check. Question: are you satisfied that intermediate GQ in a haploid organism called as diploid reflects model ambiguity rather than genuine uncertainty? If not, the whole analysis should be re-run at GQ ≥ 20 and the two compared.

[List Paragraph] Disabling the call-rate MAD rule (Section 4.2.3). Decided after seeing its effect on sample counts. Question: would you have made the same decision if the counts had gone the other way? If not, consider whether the absolute floor should instead be raised to 0.90 as a compromise.

[List Paragraph] The clonal threshold of 0.00005 (Section 4.2.4). There is no natural break in the data; the value was chosen on a reasonable but arbitrary criterion. Question: do you accept a threshold with no principled basis, or should clonal pruning be reported both with and without?

[List Paragraph] Which TreeMix model to carry forward (Section 6.7). Both m = 2 and m = 3 are recorded. Question: do you report the identifiable range only (m ≤ 2) and state that the data cannot support more, or do you report m = 3 with a caveat? The former is more defensible.

[List Paragraph] How to frame the thesis objectives given the zone/region collinearity (Section 9.2). This is the most consequential question in this list. Question: do you reframe the research question around spatial structure generally, acknowledge the confounding and proceed, or seek additional sampling?
