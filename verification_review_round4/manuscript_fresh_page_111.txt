anomalies are an artefact of that computation rather than of the data. An earlier version of this paragraph 
said that five of six values reproduced to three significant figures; that was wrong, and the corrected 
accounting follows. Recomputing every migration-edge p-value in m = 0 to 5 as a one-sided normal tail from 
the printed jackknife mean and standard error gives fifteen edges, of which thirteen carry a finite printed 
value and two print “< 2.22507 × 10⁻³⁰⁸”. That figure is exactly DBL_MIN, the smallest normal double-
precision float, and it appears at m = 3 edge 2 (z = 10.53, stable tail 3.18 × 10⁻²⁶) and again at m = 4 edge 2 (z 
= 9.33, stable tail 5.45 × 10⁻²¹). Of the thirteen finite values, ten agree with a numerically stable tail to three 
significant figures. The other three, and both sentinels, are explained — and reproduced to the last printed 
digit — by computing the upper tail as 1 − Φ(z) in double precision. Immediately below 1 the doubles are 
spaced 1.1102 × 10⁻¹⁶ apart, so Φ(z) is quantised to multiples of that spacing and the tail recovered by 
subtraction can only take those values. At z = 7.843, 8.060 and 8.038 the formulation returns exactly 
2.220446 × 10⁻¹⁵, 3.330669 × 10⁻¹⁶ and 4.440892 × 10⁻¹⁶ — which are the three printed values to every digit 
printed — against stable tails of 2.1995 × 10⁻¹⁵, 3.8147 × 10⁻¹⁶ and 4.5658 × 10⁻¹⁶. Once the true tail falls 
below half that spacing, Φ(z) rounds to exactly 1.0 and the difference is exactly zero; that happens at z = 9.33 
and z = 10.53. This is rounding and cancellation, not underflow of the tail: the true tails there are 5.45 × 10⁻²¹ 
and 3.18 × 10⁻²⁶, both far above the smallest normal double, and the “< 2.22507 × 10⁻³⁰⁸” string is how 
TreeMix prints a computed zero rather than evidence that any quantity underflowed. A direct 
complementary-error-function evaluation would avoid the subtraction entirely. The accounting is therefore 
as follows. All fifteen outcomes reproduce to three significant figures under that formulation, and twelve of 
them — ten finite values and both zeroes — reproduce at their full displayed precision. The remaining three 
are moderate p-values rather than extreme tails: at m = 2 edge 1, for instance, the formulation returns 
0.000747669 against a printed 0.000747653, a residual consistent with the printed mean and standard error 
being themselves rounded. Compatibility on this scale is strong evidence that the run computed jackknife 
standard errors, resolving a doubt raised by an earlier script snapshot lacking the -se flag. None of it changes 
a reported result: both sentinels belong to m = 3 and m = 4, neither of which is reported, and the two values 
the m = 2 choice rests on are 7.48 × 10⁻⁴ and a tail of order 10⁻¹⁶ whose exact digits affect no inference.
6.8 Genome scan for differentiation outliers
[Observed] Across the 3,916-SNP matrix, XtX ranged from 2.48 to 26.44 with a median of 3.45. Calibration 
simulated pseudo-observed SNPs under the estimated population covariance matrix, with pi.maf = 0.05 
truncating the simulated ancestral frequency rather than applying the observed data's MAF filter, so the 
pseudo-observed set is not MAF-matched (Section 5.6); this gives a 0.999-quantile threshold of 10.840. 
Nineteen SNPs (0.49 per cent) exceed it.
