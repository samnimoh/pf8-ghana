7.4 Practical significance
[Interpreted] What the neutral result licenses. Neutral common-variant differentiation between zones is 
negligible — maximum pairwise FST 0.00232, with one of six confidence intervals spanning zero (Guinea 
savanna vs transitional zone, [−0.00083, 0.00178]), and the maximum staying within 0.00204–0.00255 across 
six threshold scenarios (Table 8.3) — so a spatially sparse design is adequate for characterising neutral 
structure. Marker-frequency representativeness is a separate question, and the rebuilt resistance panel 
shows significant between-zone variation at six of the nine panel genes, including pfdhfr, pfdhps and pfaat1 
(the others being pfcrt, pfmdr1 and pfmdr2). If the question is whether a handful of sites will give a 
representative picture of the neutral genomic background, these data answer yes; if the question is whether 
those sites will give a representative picture of resistance-marker frequencies, these data answer no.
[Interpreted] What it does not license, and what the previous draft got wrong. The previous draft went 
further and concluded that "sentinel surveillance at a small number of sites is an adequate design for 
detecting emergence — the samples do not need to be spatially comprehensive to be representative". That 
claim is withdrawn. It is unsupported by anything measured here, and it is contradicted by the mechanism 
this project's own literature review sets out: selection builds connectivity that neutral demography does not, 
so a selected haplotype can sweep across a region while the neutral background stays homogeneous — and, 
symmetrically, resistance-allele trajectories can remain site-specific even under neutral panmixia. Sentinel 
surveillance measures marker frequencies, not neutral structure. Low genome-wide neutral FST says nothing 
about whether marker frequencies at one site represent the country.
[Observed] The resistance panel settles the question empirically, and the answer is no. Marker frequencies 
do differ by zone. pfaat1 S258L spans about 28 percentage points from coast to far north (0.824 to 0.544, χ² p 
= 1.89 × 10⁻¹⁸, n = 1,896 called). pfdhps G437 — the allele that was missing from the panel altogether in the 
previous draft — runs 0.964 in deciduous forest and 0.941 on the coast against 0.875 in Guinea savanna and 
0.800 in the transitional zone (χ² p = 1.2 × 10⁻⁶, n = 1,892). The pfdhfr codons are heterogeneous across the 
four zones (S108N χ² p = 9.4 × 10⁻⁷; C59R 4.8 × 10⁻⁵; N51I 2.4 × 10⁻⁴) and the IRN triple-mutant haplotype 
itself runs 0.750 in Guinea savanna, 0.788 in deciduous forest, 0.867 on the coast and 0.880 in the 
transitional zone on 25 isolates. These are all four-zone heterogeneity tests; no directional south-versus-
Guinea contrast was run, and the transitional zone's values rest on 25 isolates (§7.2). A sentinel site on the 
coast and a sentinel site in Upper East would report materially different resistance-marker profiles from the 
same panmictic parasite population. The operational implication is the reverse of the previous draft's: neutral 
structure can be characterised sparsely; marker frequencies cannot. A surveillance design intended to track 
resistance needs to span the gradient, and this analysis is evidence for rather than against that requirement.
