[Interpreted] A two-edge graph is the model these data select, and m = 2 is reported as a descriptive model 
rather than as a count of historical migration events. This narrows a question the original manuscript left 
open, but it does not close it the way a count of historical events would be closed. The plateau rule applied to 
variance explained picks m = 2 within the identifiable range and m = 3 across the full sweep, and with no 
edge p-values there was no way to choose between them; the manuscript retained both. The edge p-values 
now favour m = 2 — but they do so as a conditional test inside a graph whose topology, root and edge count 
were all fixed before the test was run, and they do not agree with the variance-explained criterion applied 
over the full sweep, which reaches 0.9997 at m = 3 and so still prefers m = 3. The two criteria therefore 
disagree, and the disagreement is settled here on identifiability grounds, which is a modelling decision and 
not a measurement. What the statistics do support is narrower: the third edge is indistinguishable from zero 
on its own jackknife standard error, four populations support roughly two edges on parameter-counting 
grounds, and m = 2 is accordingly the graph reported as a descriptive model. It is not an estimate of how 
many admixture events occurred in the history of Ghanaian P. falciparum, and no uncertainty in topology, 
root placement or edge count is propagated into it. The earlier hedging between m = 2 and m = 3 is replaced 
by a stated model choice, not by a resolution.
