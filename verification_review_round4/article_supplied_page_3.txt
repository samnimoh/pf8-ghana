molecular markers classifies variants rather than genes, so a gene-level summary cannot be mapped onto the 
validated, candidate and potential tiers (WHO 2025).
This study addresses three questions. First, how differentiated are Ghana's agro-ecological zones at common 
variants, measured as pairwise Hudson FST with block-jackknife confidence intervals and examined alongside 
ordination of the published genotype distances (Fig. 2, Table 2)? Second, does differentiation scale with 
geographic distance between populations, and is any such relationship robust to the thresholds and spatial units 
used to define the analysis set (Table 4)? Third, what are the frequencies, in isolates collected between 2009 and 
2021, of validated, candidate and potential resistance markers across the nine genes pfk13, pfdhfr, pfdhps, pfcrt, 
pfmdr1, pfmdr2, pfaat1, pfcoronin and pfap2mu, and do those frequencies vary between zones (Table 3)? A 
genome-wide scan for differentiation outliers is used to place the resistance loci in the context of the strongest 
zone-associated signals in the panel (Fig. 3).
Methods
Data source and sample selection
Genotypes were taken from MalariaGEN Pf8 (MalariaGEN 2025), a release of 33,325 samples and 12,493,205 
variants, accessed through the public Zarr store. Variants were admitted on ingest under the mask `FILTER_PASS 
& is_snp & numalt == 1`, and the GT, AD, DP and GQ fields were read at the retained sites, restricting the panel 
to biallelic SNPs that pass the release filter.
Each Ghanaian isolate was assigned to an agro-ecological zone by a spatial join of its reported collection location 
to the Ghana agro-ecological zone layer (Fig. 1), giving four zones: coastal savanna, deciduous forest, transitional 
and Guinea savanna. In total 4,905 Ghanaian isolates passed Pf8 release QC and were assignable to a zone, with 
collection years spanning 2009-2021.
Sample QC was applied as a cascade: within-host fixation index Fws >= 0.95, sample call rate >= 0.80, a 
heterozygosity outlier rule, and clonal pruning of near-identical genotypes. Mixed calls in the genome-wide 
matrix were resolved to the majority allele by read depth, giving one haploid allele per isolate.
Variant and sample filtering were ordered in two passes so that variant missingness is computed on the samples 
actually retained. A provisional pass on the pre-cut sample set retained 1,634,808 variants; 117 high-missingness 
samples were then dropped; recomputing missingness on the 1,900 retained isolates gave 1,807,331 variants at 
10% missingness or less, of which 3,916 reached minor allele frequency >= 0.05. The analysis set is therefore 
1,900 isolates by 3,916 SNPs, with mean variant missingness 2.95%. Its composition is Guinea savanna 1,334, 
deciduous forest 337, coastal savanna 204 and transitional 25 isolates, set out with region, dominant sequencing 
study and collection years in Table 1.
Because the Fws cut sets both the size and the zone composition of the isolate set, and the MAF cut sets the size 
of the SNP panel, the headline spatial results were recomputed under six threshold scenarios - the baseline, Fws 
>= 0.90, Fws >= 0.80, no Fws filter, MAF >= 0.01 and MAF >= 0.02 - loaded through one shared routine, so 
scenarios differ only in the thresholds (Table 4).
