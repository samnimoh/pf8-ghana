Population structure
Ordination used the published Pf8 genotype-distance submatrix restricted to the 1,900 analysis isolates, so all 
three embeddings act on identical input distances. Three two-dimensional embeddings were computed and 
coloured by zone: UMAP (McInnes et al. 2018), t-SNE and PCoA (Fig. 2). They were compared on 
trustworthiness, which penalises false near neighbours in the embedding; the Spearman correlation between 
original and embedded pairwise distances, which measures global rather than local preservation; and the 
silhouette coefficient with zone as the label, computed on the original distances as well to give a reference value 
independent of any embedding.
Pairwise differentiation between zones was estimated with the Hudson FST estimator (Hudson et al. 1992), 
formed as a ratio of averages across SNPs rather than as an average of per-SNP ratios. Confidence intervals at 
95% came from a block jackknife over contiguous blocks of SNPs (Table 2).
Ancestry proportions were estimated with ADMIXTURE 1.3 (Alexander et al. 2009) for K = 1-8 with 10-fold cross-
validation, on 3,385 LD-pruned SNPs obtained from the analysis set with PLINK. Cross-validation error was 
compared across K, and the minor-component proportion at the selected K was compared across zones with a 
Kruskal-Wallis test and an eta-squared effect size.
Isolation by distance
Isolation by distance was tested with the Mantel test (Mantel 1967), correlating linearised differentiation, FST/(1 
− FST), with the logarithm of great-circle distance between population centroids. Tests were run at two spatial 
levels: four zone populations giving six pairs, and seven administrative regions, the finest spatial unit in the 
release metadata, giving 21 pairs. Each used 9,999 permutations of population labels, a one-sided alternative of 
positive association, and random seed 42.
At region level, three of the 21 pairs returned a negative point estimate of FST, and these were floored to zero for 
the primary test. Because flooring compresses exactly the pairs closest to zero, the test was also run on 
uncensored estimates and both are reported. No flooring was needed at zone level. Dependence on individual 
populations was assessed by a leave-one-population-out jackknife, dropping each region in turn and 
recomputing the test on the remaining six.
Population graph
Relationships among the four zone populations were summarised with TreeMix (Pickrell and Pritchard 2012), 
fitted with 0 to 5 migration edges and with standard errors estimated by jackknife using the `-se` option. 
Variance explained was recorded at each edge count, with the weight, standard error and p-value of each edge.
Four populations cannot identify more than about two edges: the covariance matrix has few independent 
entries while free parameters grow with each edge, so additional edges absorb noise. The reported edge count 
is accordingly a model choice, not an estimate of the number of historical admixture events, and the two 
selection criteria disagree: taking the smallest graph explaining 0.998 of the variance selects three edges over 
the full 0-5 sweep, whereas two edges follow only after the sweep is capped at two on identifiability grounds.
