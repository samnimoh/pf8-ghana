Resistance markers vary geographically where neutral structure does not
The two findings sit in tension only if neutral differentiation is taken as a proxy for the spatial behaviour of every 
locus. It is not. Marker frequencies do differ among zones: pfaat1 S258L stands at 0.609 overall (n = 1,896, p = 
1.89 x 10^-18), and pfdhps G437 at 0.897 overall (n = 1,892, p = 1.17 x 10^-6), ranging from 0.964 in deciduous 
forest to 0.800 in the transitional zone (Table 3). Genome-wide FST averages drift and gene flow over thousands 
of retained variants; selection acts on single loci, and the pressures that drive resistance markers — treatment 
policy, drug deployment history, treatment-seeking behaviour, transmission intensity — vary at sub-national 
scale. A locus under local selection can therefore move much further and much faster than the genome-wide 
average, and a very low genome-wide estimate places no constraint on how far apart marker frequencies can 
be.
The surveillance implication must be drawn carefully. A very low neutral FST licenses a claim only about the 
magnitude of neutral structure: at the retained common variants, among-zone differentiation is very low, 
though five of the six intervals exclude zero. It does not license treating any single zone's marker frequency as a 
national estimate, nor does it justify sparse geographic sampling for resistance surveillance. A further caution 
applies to the zone contrasts themselves: because zone, administrative region, sequencing study and collection 
period vary together (Table 1), a significant difference among zones in marker frequency is also a difference 
among studies and years.
The pfaat1 cline
S258L shows the steepest geographic gradient in the panel, running from 0.824 in coastal savanna to 0.544 in 
Guinea savanna (Table 3), and pfaat1 (PF3D7_0629500) was the second-ranked outlier gene in the BayPass XtX 
scan, with a maximum XtX of 24.20 against a calibrated 0.999-quantile threshold of 10.840 (Fig. 3; Gautier 2015). 
Of the 19 outliers, 13 fall at q <= 0.10 under Benjamini-Hochberg correction (Benjamini and Hochberg 1995).
The functional literature on this substitution is more specific than the cline alone would suggest. Amambua-
Ngwa et al. (2023) reported an effect of S258L that was real but modest, dependent on epistasis and on genetic 
background, and directional in one place: they found that it potentiates chloroquine resistance. Amodiaquine, 
lumefantrine, mefloquine and quinine were all measured in that work and the shifts reported were sub-
threshold; the authors concluded that chloroquine was the primary selective force acting on the locus.
A contemporary drug explanation for the Ghanaian cline is therefore a hypothesis to be tested, not an inference 
that follows from an outlier statistic, and several alternatives have to be excluded first. The zones differ in 
dominant sequencing study and in collection years, so the cline is confounded with study and with period; the 
gradient may record historical rather than current chloroquine exposure; the administrative region is the finest 
spatial unit available, so the apparent zone gradient may track where sampling happened rather than ecology; 
and the codon may be a passenger on linked selected variation rather than the target. An XtX value above the 
threshold identifies a locus whose frequencies differ among populations by more than the calibrated null 
permits; it does not name the selective agent, and the pseudo-observed set used for calibration is not matched 
on minor allele frequency to the observed panel.
