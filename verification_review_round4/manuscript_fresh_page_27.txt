[Interpreted] The direction of the gradient is therefore reproduced within the two zone pairs that share a

[Interpreted] The practical reading is as follows. Study confounding does not threaten the central finding that 
the observed differentiation is very low, but neither does that finding bound the true differentiation: batch 
effects can move an observed between-group contrast in either direction — through cohort composition, 
calling differences and differential missingness — so a batch-confounded design that still returns FST ≈ 0.002 
is reporting a low observed value whose relation to the true value is not established, not an upper bound on 
it. It does threaten any positive between-zone claim — the maximum-FST pair, the polyclonality gradient, the 
ADMIXTURE minor-component ordering, and the marker-frequency differences in §6.9 — because for the 
pairs concerned there is no study-level control to appeal to. Each of those claims should be read as "differs 
between zones, which in these data means between zone, region, period and sequencing batch jointly".
What would resolve it. Nothing available within Pf8. Separating study from zone requires either isolates from the 
same study in both the coastal savanna and the Guinea savanna, or a replication cohort sequenced under one 
protocol across the gradient. Statistical adjustment is not an option: with four of six pairs at zero overlap, a study 
term and a zone term are not jointly identifiable. This is listed as design limitation 7 in §9.2 and is the one limitation 
on that list that new sequencing, rather than reanalysis, would have to fix.
