Three counts that must not be conflated. The Stage 12 retrieval panel is nine genes (Table 3.2), covering 57 codons. 
Of those 57, 24 are polymorphic in these isolates, in eight of the nine genes — pfcoronin's three codons are all fixed 
at the reference residue. And config.KNOWN_RESISTANCE_LOCI, the Stage 9 cross-reference list, is a different eight 
— pfcrt, pfmdr1, pfdhfr, pfdhps, pfk13, pfmdr2, pfcoronin, pfap2mu — which excludes pfaat1. That exclusion is the 
mechanism by which a real positive was missed, so the counts are named explicitly wherever they appear rather 
than left to the reader to disentangle.
Caveat. The cross-reference intersects gene bodies with no flanking window (--flank defaults to 0 at 
step09_annotate.py:61 and the final run applied none), so an outlier in near-complete linkage with a panel locus 
but outside its annotated span is not flagged. This is a live limitation, not a defect of the superseded run that has 
since gone away. The pfap2mu-adjacent SNP is still an outlier in the final run and is still unflagged: 
results/baypass/outlier_snps.bed lists Pf3D7_12_v3:715,788 at XtX 15.086, ninth of the 19, it is absent from 
results/outliers_annotated.txt, and PF3D7_1218300 (pfap2mu) still starts at 716,700 — the gap is still exactly 912 
bp. It is also one of the two outliers falling outside any annotated gene: the 19 outlier SNPs give 17 SNP-gene 
overlaps across 13 genes (logs/rerun_5to9.log), and the other of the two is the fifth-ranked outlier overall, XtX 20.79 
at Pf3D7_11_v3:1,054,447, q = 0.005 — which is also one of the two SNPs the quality audit downgrades (Section 
3.11.1). Naming the live pfap2mu near-miss is stronger evidence for this caveat than the gene-count arithmetic 
alone. Outlier counts and gene counts are therefore not interchangeable, and the finding is not "19 outliers in 13 
genes". Neither this stage nor Stage 8 applies LD pruning or clumping, so the per-gene outlier SNP counts in Section 
6.8 count linked sites separately; Section 3.11 and Section 6.8 state where that matters.
3.10 Stage 12 — the resistance marker panel, by reconstructed codon
What and why. Section 3.5.1 establishes that the genome-wide store cannot carry most of the canonical 
resistance codons. This stage answers the marker question directly instead, on the same 1,900 isolates and 
the same zone labels, in a parallel retrieval that leaves every existing output untouched. It is not a re-analysis 
of the genome-wide matrix and its results are not merged into it. It is the second attempt at this question. 
The first, Stage 10, identified markers by matching SnpEff's ANN_HGVS_p protein-change strings against a 
hand-written list, and an external critique showed that this could not work; Section 3.10.1 sets out why, and 
Section 3.10.2 the method that replaces it. Every marker number in this manuscript now comes from 
step12_codon_panel.py.
